## The Story

Tasti D-Lite started in 1987 in an Upper East Side kitchen. Celeste Carlesimo and her father Louis, a food technologist, worked out a soft-serve dessert that was sold as lower in fat and calories than ice cream. The first shops opened in Manhattan.

For its first twenty years the company did not franchise. It sold its mix wholesale and licensed the name to shop owners who bought their own machines. New York magazine reported eight stores in 1992. By 2002 the New York Times counted 35 locations, and by early 2005 licensed shops had opened in New Jersey, Connecticut, Florida, Maryland and Illinois. The core of the business stayed in Manhattan, where the product had a loyal following among people counting calories.

In 2007 the private equity firm SPG Partners bought the company. James H. Amos, who had grown Mail Boxes Etc. through franchising, became CEO, and headquarters moved from New York to Franklin, Tennessee. In 2008 the company launched a franchise program and signed its first international development agreement, for South Korea. Most of the existing licensed stores converted to franchises.

## What Went Wrong

The product's main selling point was the calorie count, and the count did not hold up. In 2002 the New York Times reported that Tasti D-Lite had fewer calories than ice cream but more than the company claimed. In 2005 the company settled with the New York City Department of Consumer Affairs. It paid the city $100,000, agreed to stop calling the product "low calorie," and agreed to post nutrition information for all of its flavors.

The new owners then tried to turn a Manhattan habit into a national chain. Franchise fees reportedly ran from $250,000 to about $500,000. In November 2011 the company bought Planet Smoothie, a chain of more than 100 franchised smoothie shops. At that point Tasti D-Lite reportedly had about 60 stores and commitments for 500 more. The 500 never opened.

In June 2015 the franchisor Kahala Brands bought both Tasti D-Lite and Planet Smoothie. The two brands together had 128 locations, and Planet Smoothie made up most of that count. Kahala folded both into its Scottsdale, Arizona headquarters. Over the following years most of the remaining Tasti D-Lite shops closed. By 2024 a food writer counted two left, both in Manhattan.

## The Lesson

Tasti D-Lite had a product people in one city bought every day. The plan after 2007 assumed that demand would follow the brand into new markets once franchisees paid to open stores. It did not. A local following tells you a product works in one place. It does not tell you whether strangers in another state will pay for it, and selling franchise commitments before answering that question shifts the risk to the people buying the franchises.

The calorie claims mattered as well. When the product's reason to exist is a number on the label, that number has to be right, because a regulator or a reporter with a lab will eventually check it.
